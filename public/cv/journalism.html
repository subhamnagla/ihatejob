How to write a Journalism CV

Called a resume in the US and a CV almost everywhere else — the same document, and these are the rules it gets judged by.

The conventions below are the ones this field actually uses. They are what the builder applies when you pick Journalism / Media, and what the rating checks a finished CV against.

What it must contain

Leave any of these out and the CV reads as incomplete to someone who hires in this field:

  • Work experience — Beat, output rate, and what the reporting produced - a correction, a policy change, a scoop.

The order to put them in

Order is not cosmetic. What sits at the top is what gets read before someone decides whether to keep reading.

  1. Professional summary
  2. Work experience
  3. Selected Bylines
  4. Skills
  5. Education
  6. Achievements
  7. Languages
  8. Certifications

Section by section

  • Selected Bylines — Link the pieces. Editors read the work, not the description of the work.

What to put numbers on

The single most common reason a CV in this field reads as weak is that nothing in it is measured. These are the figures that mean something here:

stories per week · readership · time on page · exclusives

Verbs that carry weight

Openers like “responsible for” and “worked on” describe a job description rather than a person. In this field these do the work instead:

Reported · Investigated · Broke · Edited · Filed · Produced

What gets one rejected

  • A portfolio or clips link is expected for editorial roles.

Length

1 to 2 pages is normal for this field.

Common questions

What should a journalism CV include?

A journalism CV has to carry work experience. The order this field expects is professional summary, work experience, selected bylines and skills.

What goes first on a journalism CV?

The professional summary. That is where this field's convention puts it, and it is what the builder applies when you pick Journalism.

How long should a journalism CV be?

1 to 2 pages is normal for this field. Going longer does not add weight; it moves the thing being looked for further down.

Is a journalism CV the same as a journalism resume?

For applying to a job, yes - the same document under two names. Resume is the usual word in North America and CV almost everywhere else. The one real exception is the academic CV, which is long-form and lists publications.
